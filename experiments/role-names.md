# Role names for the trio coverage lab

Research snapshot (2026-08-09, agent-verified against job boards, levels.fyi,
and industry writing). Feeds `ROLE_NAMES` in `trio.html` - the live table
carries a curated subset; this file is the full menu to promote from.

Flag key: ★ = one of the ~10 most universally recognized titles
(recruiter-safe vocabulary).

## Zone 1 - Product only

| Title | Depth | What it means in practice | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product Manager ★ | wide | Owns the "should we build it" question for a product area: strategy, roadmap, feature definition | common |
| Senior Product Manager | wide | PM with harder scope: complex projects, larger teams, heavier stakeholder management | common |
| Associate Product Manager (APM) | wide | Entry-level PM learning strategy/roadmapping while supporting established PMs | common |
| Growth Product Manager | deep | Owns a metric (activation, retention, conversion) rather than a product surface; experiment-driven | common |
| Product Operations (Product Ops) | deep | Makes the product org itself run: tooling, data flow, process, launch coordination | common (grew fast in the 2020s) |
| Product Marketing Manager (PMM) | deep | Positioning, launches, go-to-market - the outbound half of product | common |
| Group Product Manager (GPM) | wide | Player-coach PM: still owns product areas while managing other PMs | common |
| Director / VP of Product | wide | Runs the PM team and the portfolio-level roadmap | common |
| Chief Product Officer (CPO) ★ | wide | Executive owner of product strategy across the company | common |
| Product Analyst | deep | The data specialist inside product: metrics, funnels, experiment readouts | common |

## Zone 7 - All three circles

| Title | Depth | What it means in practice | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founding Designer ★ | wide | First design hire; sets product direction, brand, and often ships front-end - the canonical wide generalist (First Round's guide is the reference text) | common in startup vocabulary |
| Founding Engineer | wide | First engineer; builds the MVP but also does de-facto PM and UX work by necessity | common in startup vocabulary |
| Founder / Co-founder (product founder) | wide | The original all-three blob - everything is in scope until hires arrive | common |
| Head of Product (startup-sized) | wide | At small companies this seat spans product, design direction, and technical tradeoffs | common |
| Design Founder / Designer-Founder | wide | Descriptor for founders whose superpower is design; used in VC/industry writing | niche (descriptor) |

## Notes: contested titles and naming trends

- **Design Engineer is winning over UX Engineer.** Both name the D+E hybrid, but the 2024-26 momentum (Vercel, Linear, the "design engineering" wave) is behind *Design Engineer*; UXE survives mainly where it's an official ladder (Google). *Design Technologist* is the same person at Amazon. For a recruiter-facing tool, treat these three as one blob position with Design Engineer as the label.
- **Product Engineer is the fastest riser** in the P+E zone - startups increasingly skip the PM and hire engineers who own the "should we." Distinct from *Product Designer* despite the mirrored construction.
- **Product Designer displaced "UX/UI Designer"** as the default design title in US tech during the 2020s; its whole point is the P+D overlap.
- **Content Designer beat UX Writer** as the words-in-product title (Meta, Google, Shopify all renamed).
- **TPM is a genuine acronym collision** - Technical Product Manager and Technical Program Manager are different jobs sharing three letters; big tech means Program, startups usually mean Product.
- **Descriptors, not postings:** "player-coach," "front-of-the-front-end," "unicorn," "T-shaped" - real vocabulary, but they modify a title rather than being one. Useful for depth-axis language rather than blob labels.
- **Depth pattern worth encoding:** IC ladders (Staff/Principal, Staff Product Designer) generally read as *deep*; "founding," "fractional," "full-stack," "lead," "head of" prefixes read as *wide*. Seniority and width correlate in title language but are not the same axis.

## Gap -> suggested hire (seed data for the suggestion feature)

Suggestion logic: rim gaps get SPECIALISTS (deep), seam gaps get HYBRIDS
(wide, spanning two circles), center/whole-map gaps get FOUNDING or
FRACTIONAL generalists.

|---|---|---|

| Product rim | Product Marketing Manager | You keep building the right thing and telling no one. |
| Product rim, revenue edge | Pricing / Monetization Manager | The product is good and the pricing is a shrug. |
| Design core | Product Designer | Features ship, and every one fights the user. |
| Design rim | Brand Designer | The product works but doesn't look like it belongs to anyone. |
| Design rim, evidence edge | UX Researcher | Every design debate ends in opinions because nobody's asked a user. |
| Engineering core | Full-Stack Engineer | Great plans, beautiful mockups, nothing runs. |

## Temporary / rented coverage (Derek, 2026-08-09)

Not every gap is a HIRE - many are rentals, and orgs outsource exactly
the rim work above all the time. The suggestion feature should offer both
modes per gap: hire it, or rent it.

- Design rim -> branding AGENCY (huge brand pushes are classically
  outsourced - identity systems, launch campaigns)
- Product rim, market edge -> product research CONSULTANCY (initial
  market/user research before any product hire exists)
- Engineering rim -> engineering CONSULTING / contract specialists
  (security audit, infra buildout, an embedded firmware contract)
- Center / leadership -> FRACTIONAL leaders (fractional CPO/CTO/design
  leader - already a real services category)

Rentals fit gaps that are INTENSE BUT TEMPORARY (a rebrand, an audit, a
zero-to-one research push); hires fit gaps that are PERMANENT LOAD. That
distinction - spike vs. steady-state - might be the cleanest way the tool
could phrase the choice.

## Contested / trending notes (pass 2)

- Developer Advocate: the map's most interesting rim case - sits on
  Engineering's rim but faces outward at a community; being refactored
  toward "Developer Experience Engineer."
- DevOps vs Platform Engineer: same territory, older vs newer label.
- Product Owner: real in Scrum-enterprise, a synonym/demotion elsewhere.
- Data Scientist: genuinely straddles two rims - good stress test for
  position-derives-title.
- Motion Designer appears twice ON PURPOSE: brand motion (Design rim)
  vs product motion (D+E seam) - same words, different hires.
- Hottest rim demand per 2025 writing: motion design + ML engineering.
  Alive-but-declining: DBA, Network Engineer, IA, Scrum Master.
