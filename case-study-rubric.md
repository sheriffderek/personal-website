# Case study rubric (private, repo root)

A place to collect outline ideas and hold our cards against a rubric - so "is this card good?" is a comparison, not a vibe. Sources so far: our own writing rules (CLAUDE.md) and a 2026 UX-hiring video breakdown of what Fortune 500 hiring managers scan for. Add new sources as we find them; date them.

## The scan reality

Hiring managers spend 3-5 minutes TOTAL on a portfolio - not per case study. They scan headlines and visuals and gut-decide before reading a paragraph. Everything below is judged under that constraint. (This is why the weighted timeline exists: the default view IS the curated 2-4-deep cut.)

## The narrative arc (per card / per "Read more")

1. **Hook** - one sentence, the problem, not the company bio. "Users were abandoning onboarding at step three and nobody knew why" beats any founded-in-2019 setup.
2. **Role** - exactly what I did, who I worked with, the timeline.
3. **Problem statement** - framed from the user's perspective, not the business's.
4. **Approach** - methods chosen and WHY, constraints acknowledged. Rationale over checklist ("I use a knife and a stove" is not a method).
5. **Key insights** - the two or three that mattered, not all findings.
6. **Impact** - what CHANGED because of the work. Metrics if real, otherwise qualitative proof of influence (the VP paused the roadmap; the PM redesigned the flow). Proof of influence is proof of impact. Never a made-up number.
7. **Reflection** - what I'd do differently, specific, not performative. "Show us your vector of growth."

Formula: problem → approach → insight → impact → reflection. A story, not a report.

## Per-card checks (run these against every entry)

- [ ] Can a scanner get the whole pitch from title + description alone? (They never hit Read more.)
- [ ] Does it open with a hook, not context?
- [ ] Is my role distinct from the team's? (Named collaborators = crossfunctional proof. All-"I" = solo-vacuum red flag OR credit-taking red flag - no winning version.)
- [ ] Does it END on something that changed - a decision, a metric, a habit - not on "delivered recommendations"?
- [ ] Any why-this-method thinking, or just a tool list?
- [ ] Anywhere honest about mess/limits? A flawless story reads dishonest or junior.
- [ ] Not framed as "I was learning" - framed as what I delivered.

## Site-level checks

- [ ] 2-4 deep stories in the default view; judged by the weakest visible thing.
- [ ] About/intro reads like a point of view, not a LinkedIn summary. (The home page-header is our calibration example.)
- [ ] The site's own UX IS the audition - broken nav or slow load answers their biggest question the wrong way.
- [ ] The portfolio argues future value, not archives past work.

## The real question being answered

They already assume the craft (that box was checked before they opened the tab). The portfolio is auditioning for: can this person frame a messy problem, make trade-offs under constraints, influence without authority, get a room of disagreeing stakeholders to see what the data says.

## Outline ideas / open threads

- **Arc-as-carousel (2026-09-11):** one slide per step of the 7-step arc - a still that states the step (title card / big stat / before-after), press to play a short video of Derek talking through it. The existing media system already carries it: `play` items with freeze-frame posters, storyboard file naming (`01-play-wide-hook`, `02-photo-wide-role`, ...). Stills alone = the case study skimmed; videos = the dig-deeper. Mixed photo/play per step is fine - not every step earns a video.
- **The depth ladder (2026-09-11, settles the question above):** home page = the career span, organic and scannable. Rung 1: card intro (whole pitch stands alone). Rung 2: Read more (inline dig-deeper). Rung 3: a full case study - its own page on this site (where the arc-as-carousel lives) OR an external link (PE site write-up etc.) when the real artifact already lives somewhere better. Only 2-4 entries ever need rung 3 - that's the "two to four deep case studies" rule without deleting the career. Cards never carry the full 7-step arc; the arc is the rubric for rung-3 pages. Implied seam, not built: entries eventually want a "full story" pointer (internal route or external URL), distinct from `link`/`link_label` which points at the product.
- **A `/case-studies` index page (2026-09-11):** second front door to the same rung-3 pages, for the recruiter who arrived wanting "show me 2-4 deep stories" rather than the career scan. Just an index - poster cards linking to the deep pages (internal + external, labeled by where they live). Natural Pages-menu slot; `?target=` pages can link recruiters straight to it. Discipline: judged by its weakest entry, so it doesn't exist until 2+ rung-3 pages hold the rubric. Sequencing: deep pages first, index after.
- **Cadence (2026-09-11):** the site is progressively enhanced - structure first (this ladder), always presentable at every state, built into over time. Each rung-3 page ships as a complete slice with its own public moment (LinkedIn post etc.) - the announcement is the forcing function that makes the page hold the rubric, and the visible momentum is itself a signal ("this person ships"). One case study at a time, finished and announced, never four half-built.
